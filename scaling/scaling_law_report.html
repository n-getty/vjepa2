V-JEPA-2 compute-optimal scaling — an IsoFLOP study

Does V-JEPA-2 video SSL obey a Chinchilla-style compute-optimal law Nopt ∝ Cα? 20-cell IsoFLOP sweep (6 widths × budgets 3e17–1e19) + a 4-cell downward extension to 1e17, all from scratch, global batch fixed at 96 so data D and model size N are decoupled. Selector axis = representation-quality metrics scored on a fixed held-out clip "ruler" against a frozen reference encoder T*. This is a different question from Leonardo's LR-transfer track (η*-vs-width, µP) — see the last section.

What the figures show

Fig 1 (IsoFLOP curves). For each budget C, representation error vs model size N. A compute-optimal law needs each budget to trace a convex bowl whose minimum (the vertex Nopt) moves right as C grows. Our bowls are shallow and the vertices do not march right. Fig 2 (vertex vs compute). Nopt(C) for each metric — the quantity a scaling law predicts. The points scatter around a flat ~100–200M line; they do not follow a rising power law. Fig 3 (joint-fit likelihood). The profile of fit error vs the global exponent α (all cells fit at once, the statistically strongest test). The valley is broad and centered near α≈0.1, comfortably including α=0. Fig 4 (α with confidence intervals). Every metric's bootstrap α CI includes 0; the dimension-invariant metrics (CKA, mutual-kNN) also exclude the LLM value 0.5.

Primary result — honest null. Across 6 metrics × 2 reference encoders (1B ViT-g and 2B ViT-G) × per-budget and joint fits, we cannot resolve a compute-optimal exponent for V-JEPA-2 over our 1e17–1e19 (2-decade) budget range. Every α confidence interval includes 0 (best-conditioned: mutual-kNN α = +0.11, 90% CI [−0.08, +0.33]). We verified this is a compute-leverage limit, not a metric artifact: dimension-invariance (CKA), a reference-free rank metric (RankMe), and a larger 2B reference all leave the vertex flat.
The one directional signal — suggestive, not conclusive. The three dimension-invariant metrics agree on a small positive α̂ (+0.10 to +0.22) and their CIs exclude the LLM value α≈0.5. Read together: we can rule out strong LLM-style scaling, but we cannot yet distinguish weak scaling (α≈0.1–0.2) from none (α=0). The hypothesis "V-JEPA's Nopt grows more slowly with compute than LLMs" is consistent with the data but not established by it — the sign is right and strong-scaling is excluded, but the effect is not separated from zero.

Can we claim a distinct scaling law? — self-assessment

To assert "V-JEPA has a scaling law distinct from LLMs" we must first show a law exists (α CI excluding 0), then show it differs (α CI excluding 0.5). We have the second, not the first. You cannot claim a curve is a different shape before showing it is a curve. Verdict: not yet a paper. What we do have is (a) a rigorous, reproducible methodology (IsoFLOP + frozen-reference metric family + pre-registered vertex test), and (b) a well-quantified negative with a concrete power analysis for what would settle it.

What would make it a paper (power analysis). With measured per-budget vertex noise σ(logN)≈0.10 and a 5:1 SNR target, resolving α needs a lever of 5σ/α decades in compute. At the suggested α≈0.15 that is ≥3.3 decades; we have 2.0. The decisive experiment is to extend up — add 1e20 and 3e20 budgets (1e17→3e20 = 3.5 decades) — at which point even α=0.15 clears the noise floor. Cost ≈10× the 1e19 gigantic cell (a multi-day capacity run). Extending down is blocked: 3e16 is infeasible at gb=96 (step floor leaves only the tiny model above the minimum-steps threshold).

Relationship to Leonardo's LR-vs-width track

Leonardo's report (scaling/lr_ablation_figures.html) answers a different and complementary question: how the optimal learning rate η* transfers with encoder width (a µP / LR-transfer study), selecting with the same Metric-B family. His axis is η* vs width at fixed compute; ours is Nopt vs compute at tuned LR. His result is clean and interior (k ≈ −1.6, steeper than µP, corroborated by a downstream Metric-A staircase kA≈−1.2). We agree with it — and it is directly useful to us: it says larger widths need smaller LR (which our from-scratch cells must respect to avoid the collapse he documents, e.g. large@1.6e-3). The two tracks share infrastructure and the metric definition but are not in tension: LR-transfer (his) is a prerequisite for a fair compute-optimal sweep (ours). Neither is yet a standalone paper; combined ("how to tune and size V-JEPA compute-optimally") they are a stronger story, still pending the wider-budget experiment for the Nopt(C) axis.
